WASH Insecurity Analysis — composite + four pillars

WIA composite score

Aggregated WASH-insecurity score (0–1) per state, calculated as the equally weighted sum of the four dimensions. Higher scores indicate greater compound WASH insecurity from inadequate access, observed hazard impact, exposure to environmental risk, and demographic vulnerability.

Pillar 1 — Access to WASH Services

Deprivation score on the JMP service ladders (drinking water, sanitation, hygiene) plus WASH-system shocks. Reflects both the presence of infrastructure and the degree to which services meet population needs.

Pillar 2 — Hazard Impact

Observed effects of natural or human-induced shocks on WASH systems and populations over the past year — flooding, cyclones, water scarcity, extreme temperatures, displacement, cholera, and violence (each weighted 1/7 inside the pillar).

Pillar 3 — Hazard Exposure

Underlying hazard potential and long-term environmental risk — the likelihood that populations are located in areas prone to flooding, cyclones, water scarcity, extreme temperatures, or cholera (each weighted 1/5 inside the pillar).

Pillar 4 — WASH-Related Vulnerabilities

Demographic, socioeconomic and contextual factors that determine how severely a population is affected by hazards or service gaps — vaccination coverage, NTDs, diarrhoea, acute and chronic malnutrition, illiteracy and poverty prevalence (each weighted 1/7).
