2. What's driving severity

Share of households by what is driving their severity level

Around 7 in 10 households end up in their severity level because of gaps in WASH services themselves — broken or absent infrastructure, unsafe access, inadequate living standards. At scale, this is the most effective lever: investment in source rehabilitation, latrine construction, hygiene-supply pipelines, and safer access for women and children.

A further roughly 18% of households are pushed up one severity level because of the coping strategies they have already had to adopt — using unimproved water sources, sharing latrines beyond capacity, going without soap, fetching water at unsafe times. These are quiet signals that conditions are worse than infrastructure alone would suggest, and they tend to predict where things will deteriorate further if no assistance arrives.

In about 10% of cases nationally, the determining signal is WASH-related disease incidence or outbreaks observed at area level. This signal does not appear in household surveys at all; it comes from disease surveillance — confirmed cholera and acute watery diarrhoea outbreaks, elevated case rates, fatalities. At area level it lifted 6 of the 17 assessed states above what household conditions alone would have indicated, and it is the only mechanism that can move an area into the catastrophic level without a corresponding household-level signal. Response design in those 6 states therefore needs outbreak control and water-quality measures alongside service-gap programming.
